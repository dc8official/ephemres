# Release Notes: Ephemres v3.3.6

**Release Tag**: `v3.3.6`  
**Target Architecture**: Vertical Integration Architecture — a rebuilt Traffic page, sFlow and SNMP polling, sites and tags, per-account sessions, and the first-phase review hardening  
**Status**: Release candidate on branch `v3.3.6`. The date is set when the tag is made, after the lab tests listed under "What was not tested".  
**Repository**: `https://github.com/dc8official/ephemres.git`  

---

## Executive Summary

Ephemres `v3.3.6` keeps the zero-dependency core of `v3.3.5` (the Python Standard Library plus `uvicorn`, `httptools`, `uvloop` and `asyncpg`, installed from a hash-checked lock) and changes what operators see and what the server accepts from the network:

1. **A Traffic page built around each WAN link now**: the last five minutes of every WAN link against its capacity, with the range that is usual for that hour, today's peak and open alerts; one chart per link; the shared site/location/tag filter and a device picker; each conversation counted once everywhere.
2. **sFlow v5**: switches and hosts that only speak sFlow report to a new UDP 6343 listener, each sample scaled by its own sampling rate.
3. **SNMP polling** (`ephemres-snmpd`, its own service): shared encrypted credential sets, interface names, speeds and status, per-minute interface counters, LLDP/CDP neighbours on the map, and WAN link-down alerts. BER, SNMP v2c and v3 are hand-written.
4. **Sites, locations, tags and one shared filter** on the dashboard, topology map, reports and Traffic page, with bulk assignment.
5. **Per-account sessions**: an idle timeout and a session limit per account, an admin 12-hour cap, and the reason a session ended.
6. **Route discovery that follows failover**: a current and a normal path per endpoint, a route-change log, new triggers (transit device down, reply TTL), and parallel traces.
7. **Traffic alerts on WAN links**, opt-in per channel.
8. **First-phase review fixes** (15 November 2026): flowd no longer stops on one bad flow record, long Traffic windows answer in seconds, the page says what it could not load, a cross-site page can no longer change anything with a signed-in session, and a router with a wrong clock no longer misplaces its traffic.
9. **Upgrades that land the release** and never leave monitoring down.

## Key Feature Highlights

### 1. Traffic Page (`/bandwidth`)
* **Now: each WAN link against its capacity** (`GET /bandwidth/wan-links`): In and Out bars against the link's speed, the usual range for this hour (from the last 28 days of hourly averages, in the server's timezone), today's peak, SNMP state and any open traffic alert. A link with no speed shows its rates and, for admins, **Set speed**.
* **One chart per WAN link**, in % of capacity with a 100% line, grouped by site; links with trouble open first. "All links together" keeps the combined chart.
* **Scope**: every read takes the shared filter and an optional device. SNMP-only routers are in the picker; unregistered flow sources are one line with Review, Enroll and Link.
* **Counted once, one meaning of "internal"**: a conversation two routers report counts once, at the larger report, in Applications, QoS, zones and Conversations alike; internal means a private address or a monitored endpoint.
* **Conversations**: request and reply are one row (host and peer, what each side sent, suspicious rule judged per direction), with the sites of the routers that saw it, first and last seen, and a name for outside addresses: a provider (Microsoft 365, Teams, Google, AWS, CloudFront, Cloudflare, Zoom) from published ranges shipped with the release, or reverse DNS.
* **Interfaces across devices**: WAN links first, then the busiest, shut-down ports last; Now in/out beside the window's average, P95 and peak; status from SNMP with the down time spelled out. The page asks for the rows it shows (WAN links and the 20 busiest) and fetches the rest on "Show all".
* **Updates**: once a minute at 15 seconds past, not while the tab is hidden; the window is remembered.

### 2. sFlow v5 and Flow Hardening
* **sFlow**: `monitoring/flow/sflow_parser.py` reads flow samples (raw headers or pre-decoded IPv4/IPv6 records) and scales each by the rate in its own sample. Counter samples are skipped (interface counters come from SNMP). The Traffic page says its figures are estimates while any exporter sends sampled traffic. `upgrade.sh` opens UDP 6343 when flow ingestion is on.
* **Records are checked before they are summed**: bytes or packets above 2^50, a protocol above 255 or a port above 65535 are left out (counted, logged once a minute). When the database still refuses a write for a value in it, the batch is written again in halves and only the refused rows are dropped. Before, one such record stopped flowd storing until it was restarted.
* **Exporter clocks**: NetFlow v5/v9 and IPFIX flows are dated by the router's clock; when it is more than 2 minutes from the arrival time, the flow's times are moved by the difference and the offset is logged once an hour. A start in the future or over a day old is stored at arrival.
* **Sampling multiplier** accepts 1 to 1,000,000.

### 3. SNMP Polling (`ephemres-snmpd`, migrations `0026`–`0028`)
* **Poller**: status every 30 seconds to 5 minutes, the interface list on change and every 15 minutes, a full probe on first contact, reboot and daily. Shared credential sets with a Test button; secrets are encrypted at rest and write-only through the API.
* **Interface traffic from counters** (`0027`): per-minute deltas in `snmp_interface_minute_rollups` (7 days) with an hourly continuous aggregate (365 days); counters win over flows for the minutes that have them. Names and speeds come from SNMP unless typed.
* **Neighbours** (`0028`): LLDP and CDP link devices; the map places endpoints traceroute cannot place, root-cause analysis names a down parent, and WAN link up/down events become alerts.
* **Any-vendor SNMPv3** (AES-128/192/256, DES, 3DES, both key-stretching methods), per-device profiles that skip what a device drops, and `python -m monitoring.snmp.survey <host>` to see what a device answers. See [SNMP guide](../snmp-guide.md).

### 4. Sites, Tags and the Shared Filter (`0023`)
* `endpoints.site` and `endpoints.tags` with autocomplete; one server-side filter (`services/endpoint_filter.py`) behind `GET /endpoints`, `/endpoints/match`, the fleet summary, the PDF, the CSV export and the Traffic page.
* The filter bar keeps its state in the URL, so a branch's view can be bookmarked. Reports accept the filter or a hand-picked list. Dashboard selection can set a site, location or tags in bulk (`POST /endpoints/bulk-update`) and Settings → Organization renames or merges values (audited, admin only).
* Settings are regrouped into five tabs by task, with one Save / Discard bar.

### 5. Per-Account Sessions (`0022`)
* An idle timeout per account (admin 15, 30 or 60 minutes; viewer 30 minutes to 4 hours) counted from real interaction reported through `POST /auth/activity`; polling and the event stream do not count. Admin sessions also end 12 hours after sign-in.
* A session limit per account (1–10), and the reason a session ended (`X-Session-End-Reason`), which the sign-in page explains. Admins can list and end an account's live sessions.
* The upgrade signs nobody out and shortens no viewer's sign-in by surprise.

### 6. Route Discovery and Topology (`0024`)
* **Current and normal path** per endpoint: a failover path no longer becomes "normal" silently (a path held 24 hours does, unless pinned). Root-cause analysis compares a failure with the path in use.
* **Route-change log** with Make normal / Pin / Unpin for admins; the map draws an unused normal path faded and labels "alternate path since …" on every worker.
* **Triggers**: a transit device going down or coming back re-traces the endpoints behind it; a change of reply TTL (IPv4 and IPv6, with or without raw sockets) triggers a trace. Transit is recognised from SNMP addresses and from the addresses a device exports flows from.
* **Parallel traces** (`discovery_parallel`, `discovery_per_router`) and a rolling re-trace schedule in place of the midnight run.

### 7. Traffic Alerts on WAN Links (`0025`)
* Busy-link alerts (default 80% for 15 minutes, per link and direction) and silent-link alerts. Rules default in Settings → Alerts and can be overridden or switched off per link. New severities are opt-in per channel: an empty severity list does not include them.

### 8. First-Phase Review (15–16 November 2026)
A review of the branch with real tools before the lab tests found, and the branch now fixes:

| Finding | What changed |
| :--- | :--- |
| One out-of-range flow record stopped flowd | Records are checked at intake; a refused write is retried in pieces. |
| 24-hour and 7-day Traffic views slow or timing out; three tabs starved the database | Top talkers no longer computes a list the page does not show; one conversations statement; hourly interface tables on large fleets; the six heaviest Traffic queries take turns, a quarter of each API worker's pool, at least 2. 24 h top talkers 14.8 s to 2.2 s, interfaces 8.2 s to 0.4 s on 570,000 conversation rows a day. |
| Traffic page kept another period's figures when a call failed | Each part loads on its own; a notice names what failed, with Try again. |
| A form on another site could create an admin | A change sent from a page at another address answers `403` (`SameOriginWritesMiddleware`); see "Before you upgrade". |
| Reverse-DNS lookups shared the API's thread pool | Their own 8 threads; a dead resolver no longer delays other background work. |
| An exporter's clock was trusted | Corrected against arrival time (above). |
| 935 KB interfaces payload on every update | The page asks for the rows it shows (16 KB). |
| `%00` or a 20-digit page number gave a 500 | `400` / `422` before any query. A sweep of 4,572 hostile requests over the 39 GET routes returns no 5xx. |
| No Content-Security-Policy | Sent by the shipped nginx template (new installs; see below). |

### 9. Upgrades That Land the Release
From the upgrade test of 14 November 2026: `upgrade.sh` fetches its own release (`EPHEMRES_BRANCH` overrides), hands over to the fetched release's script, prepares everything (frontend build, hash-checked packages) before any service stops, starts the services again if it fails before the new code is copied in, stages in `/var/lib/ephemres` instead of `/tmp`, and refuses a downgrade. `backend/app/version.py` is now the one place the version is written.

## Upgrade & Migration Runbook

### From v3.3.5, v3.3.2.v or v3.3.0
Run **v3.3.6's own script**. The `upgrade.sh` those releases installed cannot do this upgrade properly: it never installs `ephemres-snmpd`.

```bash
git clone --depth 1 --branch v3.3.6 https://github.com/dc8official/ephemres.git ~/ephemres-v3.3.6
sudo bash ~/ephemres-v3.3.6/deploy/upgrade.sh
```

Clone into a directory that allows running programs (your home directory is fine). The script takes a pre-upgrade SQL backup in `/var/backups/ephemres/`, applies migrations `0022` through `0028` (from a netmon-layout v3.2.0 or v3.3.0 server it moves the install to the Ephemres layout first), installs and starts `ephemres-snmpd`, and opens UDP 6343 when flow ingestion is on. Historical data is kept, and open sessions survive. See the [deployment guide](../deployment.md#3-upgrading-to-version-336-zero-historical-data-loss) for each step.

### From v3.3.6 on
```bash
sudo EPHEMRES_BRANCH=v3.3.7 bash /opt/ephemres/deploy/upgrade.sh
```

### Before you upgrade
1. **The server needs a correct clock (NTP).** Flow times are now checked against it ([flow guide §2.7](../netflow-guide.md)).
2. **Changes are only accepted from the server's own page.** A page served from another address than the API needs that address in `[api] allowed_origins`; scripts and `curl` are not affected. A server behind the shipped nginx site under one address needs nothing. See [Security §6.A](../security.md) and [Troubleshooting §2.M](../troubleshooting.md).
3. **The Content-Security-Policy header is not added to an nginx site you already have.** Copy the `add_header Content-Security-Policy` line from `deploy/nginx.conf.template` into your site's `location /` block, then `sudo nginx -t && sudo systemctl reload nginx` ([deployment guide](../deployment.md#after-upgrading-to-v336-the-content-security-policy-header)).
4. **A development checkout** (`npm run dev`) needs the Vite proxy to keep the page's Host (`changeOrigin: false` in `frontend/vite.config.js`, already set).

## What was not tested

* **GNS3 and device tests are still to come.** sFlow from a software source (hsflowd or a lab switch), SNMP against real devices, and flows from real routers have not been run; the parsers were checked with hand-made datagrams and 240,000 mutated ones, and SNMP against Cisco and MikroTik devices during development.
* **Debian 12**: the upgrade and install were run on Ubuntu 24.04 only.
* **Upgrade paths run**: v3.3.5 to v3.3.6 and v3.3.0 (netmon layout) to v3.3.6, in a systemd container. v3.2.0 was not run on its own.
* **Traffic query limits**: the heavy Traffic views were measured on a seeded week of 570,000 conversation rows a day with a database pool of 20 (5 query slots). An installed server's API workers have a pool of 10, so 2 slots each; that was not repeated. On that data the 7-day conversations view is at the 10-second statement limit, and the seeded conversations are nearly all distinct per minute, which is heavier than real traffic.
* **Reverse-DNS isolation** was tested with a simulated resolver that never answers, not a real dead one.
* **Content-Security-Policy**: checked on ten pages of the built frontend and accepted by `nginx -t`; PDF and CSV downloads and the logo upload were not exercised under it.

Test status at this point: 1,558 tests pass on Python 3.12 and on 3.14 with TimescaleDB.
